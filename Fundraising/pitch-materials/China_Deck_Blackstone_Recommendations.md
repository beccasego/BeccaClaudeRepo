## The arbitrage — why the spread exists (second new slide)

State the thesis in one line: *China's RLT boom has flooded the market with cheap assets; Sego buys the few good ones and exits them at Western multiples.*

You already have the visuals for this on slides 4 and 5 (BFC). Add one line to each tying the picture to the buy thesis; don't rebuild them.
- (i) Supply glut: ~290 clinically staged programs by 2025, 376 preclinical (slide 4). Confirm the "~6 → ~70 companies" count with BFC and add it as the headline.
- (ii) Quality dispersion: slide 5's modality map already shows peptide-focused Lu-177 players vs. ADC entrants moving into antibody-based RLT. Say it plainly: the ADC-swap cohort is the low-quality supply; the peptide re-engineers are the buy list.
- (iii) Capability gap: Chinese biotechs lack ex-China development, regulatory, and Western BD access. A de-risked asset is worth far more in Sego's hands. This gap is precisely what Blackstone's capital funds Sego to exploit.

Why the exit is rich: $11B across 6 RLT acquisitions in 3 years; large pharma still short of high-quality assets.

The arbitrage = buy on Chinese supply-glut pricing, sell on Western scarcity pricing, with Sego closing the capability gap in between.
