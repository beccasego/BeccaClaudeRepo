## 1. Mission and Vision

## 2. Four Differentiating Engines

Sego's investor narrative is anchored to four value-driving engines that address specific, named gaps in how RLT is developed today.

### Engine 1: Bespoke RLT Target Selection

**Gap addressed:** Industry selects RLT targets by importing ADC/TCE/CAR-T validated proteins. PSMA and SSTR2 succeeded in RLT on biology that differs from what made them attractive in other modalities.

**Sego's approach:**
- Developed a standardized rubric evaluating targets across four pillars: expression, biology, feasibility, and landscape.
- 40 targets screened, 12 deep-diligenced (8 high, 4 medium priority), 6 selected for prosecution.
- Novel selection criterion: "tumor-specific accessibility." In healthy epithelium, targets like CEACAM5, GCC, CLDN1, and MUC1-C are shielded by apical localization or tight-junction sequestration. Malignant transformation causes loss of polarization, exposing the target on the tumor cell surface while healthy tissue remains inaccessible.
- For prostate targets (ACP3, KLK2), the criterion is tumor-restricted expression with minimal or absent expression in dose-limiting organs (salivary gland, kidney).

### Engine 2: Harmonized Pharmacology

**Gap addressed:** Narrow focus on optimizing ligand affinity at the expense of PK, target biology, and isotope characteristics.

**Sego's approach:**
- Optimize the whole molecule (ligand + linker + chelator + metal) from start of screening.
- Proprietary cyclic peptide screening platform: 45B-member library, pooled ASMS-based screening, capacity for 6 programs annually.
- Advance 3 to 5 diverse candidates per target into FIH (not a single molecule). Candidates are selected to have varied preclinical profiles (different epitopes, affinities, plasma half-lives) to maximize probability of success in humans.
- Quantitative go/no-go thresholds at each stage gate:
  - Tumor absorbed dose: >100 Gy cumulative (goal), >80 Gy (minimum)
  - Tumor:kidney ratio: >3
  - Tumor:red marrow ratio: >20
  - Kidney: ≤23 Gy cumulative
  - Red marrow: ≤2 Gy cumulative
- ICP-MS-based cold-metal in vivo screening enables rapid biodistribution assessment before committing to hot radiolabeling.

### Engine 3: Clinical Translation (Bench to Bedside and Back)

**Gap addressed:** Missed opportunities to utilize FIH imaging studies to accelerate development. Mice are poor predictors of human biodistribution.

**Sego's approach:**
- Phase 0 FIH imaging studies as the primary decision tool: bring 3 to 5 candidates into human imaging to generate real dosimetry data before selecting a development candidate.
- Cost premium of multi-candidate FIH is approximately 30% over single-candidate, adds approximately 3 months. Dramatically improves probability of selecting a winner before GMP scale-up and full tox.
- eIND regulatory pathway in the US; streamlined paths in South Africa and Australia (no GLP tox requirement for microdose imaging).
- Global clinical trial network: >5 sites across US, EU, South Africa, Australia. Master protocol approach.
- "In silico Phase 1" dosimetry software in development: voxel-level time-activity curves, organ-at-risk clearance prediction, DLT estimation, Phase 1 dose justification. Aligned to FDA August 2025 draft guidance on dosimetry-driven RLT.
- Feed learning from clinic back to research. Human data from Phase 0 informs future program design.

### Engine 4: Integrated Supply Chain

**Gap addressed:** Complexity in isotope logistics causes delays, batch failures, increased COGS. Isotopes decay during transit.

**Sego's approach:**
- 67K sq ft facility in Salt Lake City housing research, development, and cGMP manufacturing under one roof. First radiopharmaceutical facility to integrate all three.
- 0.5 miles from Nusano (isotope supply), 4 miles from SLC airport.
- Phased facility build: Stage I-A (research + radiochem + vivarium + initial GMP, completion Dec 2026), Stage I-B (R&D expansion, 2028-2029), Stage II (commercial GMP, gated on Ph1 data), Stage III (additional commercial lines, gated on Ph2 data).
- Valuation precedent: Vertically integrated radiopharma biotechs with CFR 211 manufacturing commanded ~4x upfront valuation premium in M&A (avg $2.9B vs $656M upfront for those without).

## 3. Pipeline: Six Targets

### Prostate Cancer

**ACP3 (Prostatic Acid Phosphatase) — BIC positioning**
- Indication: mCRPC initially, with intent to expand to earlier lines
- Expression: ~95% of prostate cancers positive (IHC), maintained across Gleason grades and metastases. Expression restricted to prostate in healthy tissue (no salivary gland, no kidney).
- Biology: Transmembrane form (TM-PAP) constitutively internalized via lysosomal targeting sequence. Secreted form (sPAP) has short serum half-life (1.1-2.6 hr), minimizing off-target risk. Known phosphatase active site amenable to small molecule and peptide targeting.
- Landscape: One competitor, OncoACP3 (BMS/RayzeBio, Ph 1). Key liability: does not internalize, limiting efficacy with alpha/Auger isotopes. Licensed from Philochem for $350M+.
- Sego differentiation: Internalizing cyclic peptide with 225Ac or 161Tb. Aims to displace Pluvicto head-to-head on efficacy and safety (no salivary/kidney DLTs). PSMA-RLT TAM ~$2B; ACP3-RLT TAM could be >2x.
- Human imaging data (SNMMI 2025): ACP3 shows greater bone metastasis uptake, no salivary gland uptake, less kidney/liver retention vs PSMA.
- Modality: Monomeric or bis-peptide RLT

**KLK2 (Kallikrein-related Peptidase 2) — BIC positioning**
- Indication: mCRPC initially, expanding to earlier lines
- Expression: Overexpressed in ~85% of prostate cancers. More homogeneously expressed in earlier stages (LPC, mHSPC) vs PSMA. More frequently overexpressed in bone and lymph node metastases vs PSMA. Expression increases with AR reactivation (while PSMA often declines), positioning KLK2 for PSMA-resistant disease.
- Biology: Serine protease, known active site with small molecule ligands. Cell surface retained. No native internalization, but antibody binding induces internalization. Sego will select for internalizing peptides. Secreted at very low levels.
- Landscape: One competitor, JNJ-6420 (225Ac-mAb RIT, Ph 1). Key liability: 2 Grade 5 ILD events, likely antibody-format driven (long circulation, Fc biology, cumulative lung dose), not target-specific. JNJ data actually validates KLK2 as a target while proving the antibody format is the problem.
- Sego differentiation: Peptide-based approach eliminates IgG-driven ILD risk. Rapid clearance, no Fc interaction.
- Modality: Monomeric or bis-peptide RLT

### Colorectal Cancer

**CEACAM5 (Carcinoembryonic Antigen) — FIC positioning**
- Indication: Stage II/III CRC, post-surgery MRD+ patients replacing chemo adjuvant therapy
- Expression: 90-100% of CRC, also expressed in PDAC (60%), gastric, lung, medullary thyroid (>95%). In healthy tissue, restricted to apical surface of GI epithelium (inaccessible from blood). Tumor-specific accessibility via loss of polarity.
- Biology: GPI-anchored glycoprotein (180 kDa). Slow constitutive turnover (~14.5 hr). Soluble CEA shed into serum but mass-balance analysis shows 100 ug peptide dose (~50 nmol) vastly exceeds circulating sCEA (~50 pmol), providing substantial buffering. Membrane-proximal A3-B3 domain is the preferred binding epitope (induces internalization).
- Landscape: No industry-led CEACAM5 RLT. ADC programs (Sanofi Ph3 terminated, Merck KGaA Ph3 planned H1 2026 with 31% ORR in 2L+ CRC) confirm target validity but toxicities are payload-driven. Academic M5A RIT (Ac-225 Ph1, Cu-64 immunoPET) shows clean tumor uptake with no on-target off-tumor signal.
- Sego differentiation: First peptide RLT for CEACAM5. Radiation payload avoids ILD, ocular tox, chemo-driven hematotox.
- Modality: Monomeric or bis-peptide RLT

**GCC (GUCY2C, Guanylate Cyclase C) — FIC positioning**
- Indication: Stage II/III CRC, post-surgery MRD+ patients replacing chemo adjuvant therapy
- Expression: Strong positivity in 70-98% of CRC tumors. Maintained in liver metastases in 81% of patients. Also overexpressed in gastric, esophageal, pancreatic tumors. Healthy tissue: apical in GI tract, shielded from blood.
- Biology: Transmembrane receptor with endogenous peptide ligands (guanylin, uroguanylin). FDA-approved peptide agonists exist for constipation (linaclotide, plecanatide), validating peptide ligandability. Rapidly internalized into lysosomes, ligand-independent. No secretion.
- Landscape: No active industry GCC RLT. ADC and bispecific programs failed due to narrow therapeutic window (Takeda ADCs discontinued, Pfizer TCE discontinued). CAR-T programs show efficacy (~73.7% DCR) but with GI toxicity and CRS. RLT avoids immune-mediated toxicities and apical restriction provides expanded therapeutic window.
- Key investor concern: Takeda GCC discontinuation. Sego's answer: ADC/TCE failures were modality-driven (limited TI, payload constraints). RLT's first-pass kinetics, rapid clearance, and lack of immune activation create a wider window.
- Modality: Active or allosteric site targeted peptide RLT

### Lung / Multi-Cancer

**CLDN1 (Claudin-1) — FIC positioning**
- Indication: 2L metastatic sqNSCLC, with potential to combine with PD-L1. Also applicable to HNSCC, cervical squamous, CRC, HCC.
- Expression: Overexpressed in 77% lung squamous, 75% HCC, 49% HNSCC, 80% CRC. In healthy tissue, CLDN1 is present (skin, liver, kidney, bladder) but fully occupied in tight junctions. Tight junctions have an effective diameter of 0.6 nm, restricting access to molecules >400 Da. Peptide RLT (>1500 Da) cannot reach CLDN1 in intact junctions.
- Biology: Transmembrane tight-junction protein. In tumors, overexpression and loss of polarization expose CLDN1 outside junctions, creating the therapeutic window. Constitutively internalized. No shedding.
- Landscape: No CLDN1 RLT in development. Alentis Therapeutics has 2 CLDN1 ADCs in Ph 1/2 (MMAE and Topo-I payloads); no on-target DLTs to date. Claudin family validated by zolbetuximab (CLDN18.2 mAb, approved for G/GEJ).
- Sego differentiation: Peptide RLT with rapid clearance adds an additional safety layer over mAb approaches. Will target ECL1 or ECL2 extracellular loop regions.
- Modality: Cyclic peptide RLT

**MUC1-C (Mucin 1, C-terminal subunit) — FIC positioning**
- Indication: 2L metastatic LUAD, with potential to combine with PD-(L)1. Also applicable to breast, ovarian, renal.
- Expression: MUC1-C is overexpressed in ~87% of NSCLC without actionable driver mutations, 94% TNBC, 100% ovarian. In healthy tissue, MUC1 is apically localized on secretory epithelial cells. Malignant transformation causes: (i) loss of polarization, (ii) overexpression, (iii) shedding of MUC1-N, (iv) exposure of MUC1-C on entire cell surface.
- Biology: Heterodimeric transmembrane mucin. MUC1-N (N-terminal, shed, circulates as CA15-3/CA27.29) vs MUC1-C (C-terminal, membrane-anchored, signaling-competent). Sego targets MUC1-C specifically, not full-length MUC1. MUC1-C is natively internalized. No secretion of MUC1-C itself. The anchored C-terminal subunit provides intrinsic specificity analogous to targeting the proteolytically exposed "nub" of MUC16.
- Landscape: No MUC1-C RLT in development. CAR-T (Poseida, Minerva), ADCs (Biocytogen, Daiichi, Merck/Sutro) in clinical development. Key safety signal across all modalities: no on-target epithelial toxicity. ADC toxicities are payload-driven (ILD from DXd, hematotox from MMAE).
- Dual discovery strategy: (i) rapidly derive binders exploiting MUC1-N/MUC1-C interaction interface, (ii) de novo screening with recombinant MUC1-C.
- Modality: MUC1-C peptide RLT

## 4. Modality: Why Cyclic Peptides

Cyclic peptides offer the ideal balance for RLT:
- High specificity and target affinity, driving high tumor uptake
- Short biological half-life (~10s of minutes): first-pass kinetics restrict off-tumor binding
- Engineerable for internalization, rapid clearance, reduced kidney retention
- No Fc-gamma receptor interaction (avoids antibody-driven bone marrow toxicity)
- Non-saturating dosing (vs antibodies at saturating concentrations)
- Regulatory route through CDER (same as small molecules)
- Strong RLT precedent: both approved RLTs (Lutathera, Pluvicto) use peptide/small molecule vectors

Contrast with alternatives:
- Antibodies: long half-life (days-weeks), Fc biology, saturating doses, ILD risk, liver clearance. Antibody-based RLT programs have generated failures (JNJ KLK2 ILD).
- Small molecules: limited to targets with clear binding pockets/enzymatic sites. Lower affinities, higher Koff rates, rapid clearance reduces tumor retention.
- Antibody fragments/mini-proteins: high kidney retention that cannot be engineered out without compromising tumor uptake.

## 5. Isotope Strategy

**Primary isotope: 225Ac (alpha emitter)**
- High LET, ~50-85 um path length
- Four alpha emissions per decay chain
- 9.9-day half-life
- Default across pipeline unless expression or disease burden profile pulls toward 161Tb

**Secondary isotope: 161Tb (beta + Auger emitter)**
- Beta comparable to 177Lu (crossfire for bulky tumors)
- Auger electrons deliver short-range, densely ionizing damage for micrometastases and CTCs
- ~2-2.5x per-GBq tumor absorbed dose vs 177Lu
- Responses demonstrated in 177Lu-refractory patients (VIOLET trial)
- Gamma emissions enable SPECT imaging for dosimetry
- Used when: (i) on-target/off-tumor expression profile creates unfavorable alpha TI, or (ii) disease burden is mixed (bulky + micromets + CTCs)

**Chelator strategy:** Off-the-shelf DOTA/DOTAGA. Single, robust multi-radionuclide chelator across imaging (68Ga) and therapy (177Lu, 161Tb, 225Ac) to minimize CMC complexity.

**Isotope supply:** Multiple contracted suppliers per isotope. Strategic proximity to Nusano (225Ac, 161Tb supply coming online 2027). University of Utah (23 min away) supplies research-quantity 161Tb.

## 6. Discovery Workflow and Stage Gates

Target ID → Hit ID → Hit-to-Lead (H2L) → Lead Optimization (LO) → FIH Enabling → FIH Imaging → IND Enabling → Ph 1/2+

Key stage-gate exit criteria:

| Stage | Funnel | Key Exit Criteria |
|---|---|---|
| Hit ID | 1000s → 40-80 | Multiple series with ≤1 uM Kd/EC50, SAR trends, selectivity |
| H2L | 40-80 → 5-10 | Full molecule Kd <10 nM (biochem + cells), Ksol >100 uM, plasma stability >120 min, internalizes |
| LO | 5-10 → 3-5 | Acceptable ICP-MS tumor/organ uptake, target-dependent tumor uptake, in vivo dose response, dosimetry confirms RLT-likeness |
| FIH Enabling | 3-5 | Stability, radiolabeling efficiency, tumor:organ AUC ratios |
| FIH Imaging | 3-5 → 1 DC | SPECT tumor dose ≥80 Gy, tumor:kidney >3, tumor:marrow >20 |

Preclinical cost per program: ~$2.6M (Hit ID $600K, Cold Opt $1M, Hot Opt $500K, IND Enablement $500K). FIH + Ph1/2 per program: ~$20M total ($2.4M FIH, $18M Ph1/2).

## 7. Clinical Strategy

**Dosimetry-based patient selection:** Three-layer framework:
1. Qualifying lesion uptake on imaging companion (SUVmean-based, tumor > reference organ)
2. Individualized predicted absorbed dose threshold from Phase 0 (>100 Gy tumor, kidney ≤23 Gy, marrow ≤2 Gy)
3. Per-patient verification of population-level TI thresholds

**Theranostic pairing:** Co-develop matched imaging diagnostic integrated from FIH onward. Single regulatory filing can secure both therapy patient selection label and standalone diagnostic for staging.

**Prior RLT patients:** Included (not excluded). Cumulative dose accounting framework. Especially relevant for post-Pluvicto mCRPC relapse.

**Combination strategy:** RLT has orthogonal, physics-based MOA with minimal overlapping toxicity vs IO, ADCs, BiTEs, TKIs. Combinations with hormone therapy, PARPi, biologics from Ph1b/2 expansion. Chemo combinations require deliberate sequencing.

**Alternative dosing:** Fractionated, BCR-triggered, MRD-triggered approaches viewed as patient-selection strategies that manifest as schedule variations. The VISION 6-cycle fixed schedule reflected regulatory pragmatism, not biological optimization.

## 12. RLT Market Context

- RLT makes up only 2% of oncology clinical trials, concentrated in 2 tumor types (41% prostate, 18% neuroendocrine). Massive whitespace.
- Pluvicto peak sales >$5B. Lutathera approved 2018, Pluvicto approved 2022. Together validated the modality.
- 6 major acquisitions in 3 years: RayzeBio ($4.1B, BMS), Fusion ($2.4B, AstraZeneca), Mariana ($1.75B, Novartis), POINT ($1.4B, Eli Lilly), plus others.
- Large pharma facing patent cliff ($230B revenue loss by 2030), 28% of FDA approvals originate from large pharma. Innovation is acquired, not built internally.
- RLT vs ADC: RLT delivers physics-based cytotoxicity (DNA damage independent of cell cycle, no linker instability, no payload efflux). Paired imaging diagnostic enables prospective measurement of biodistribution and dosimetry. More predictable development model.
- RLT vs EBRT: RLT targets molecular biology (not just anatomy). Excellent micrometastasis coverage where EBRT has poor coverage. Requires knowledge of tumor biology vs tumor location.

## 13. Writing and Communication Style

All external Sego prose follows the Sego Writing Style (see sego-writing-style skill):
- First sentence fully answers the question.
- Quantitative thresholds introduced in first paragraph.
- Comparative framing (RLT vs ADC/EBRT, peptides vs antibodies).
- Confident, precise, non-defensive tone.
- Roman numerals for in-text lists: (i), (ii), (iii).
- No em dashes. Use periods, commas, colons, semicolons.
- Distinguish preclinical vs clinical evidence explicitly.
- Distinguish imaging vs therapeutic relevance explicitly.
- Cite primary sources. Flag gaps rather than fabricating citations.
