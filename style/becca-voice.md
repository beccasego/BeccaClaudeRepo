# Becca Voice

Becca's voice for internal, first-person writing. The reader is a colleague who already
has context: Jeevan, the team, a partner contact. Write the way Becca writes, not the
way an AI drafts.

Two things this skill is NOT:
- Not for external-facing Sego prose. That is answer-first, comparative, built for a
  skeptical investor. Hand off to `sego-writing-style`.
- Not a persuasion register. Internal comms inform and coordinate. They do not sell.

When unsure which skill applies: if the audience is inside Sego or the message is signed
by Becca to someone she knows, use this one.

---

## §1 — The core of the voice

Becca writes to be understood fast by someone who is busy and already knows the players.
Four properties define it:

1. **Understated.** Conviction-signaling that reads as trying too hard gets cut. State
   the thing, let it stand. No "I'm confident that," no "this is a huge win," no
   selling. When Becca rejected intern candidates, she pulled specific reasons back to a
   plain "after careful consideration, we've decided not to move forward." That instinct
   (say less, stay neutral) is the default.

2. **Structured by default.** Sub-bullets over paragraphs. Named buckets over prose
   walls. Lead with the outcome, drop the process. A three-line update beats a paragraph
   that buries the point in the middle.

3. **Filtered.** Becca dictates stream-of-consciousness and expects the draft to sort
   signal from noise. Tracker-level detail (specific VC conversation notes, financial
   modeling minutiae, presentation logistics) does not belong in an email to Jeevan. Cut
   it or move it. When in doubt about whether a detail earns its place, it does not.

4. **Warm only where earned.** Neutral is the baseline. Warmth is deliberate and
   relationship-specific: Becca differentiated one intern rejection to be warm because
   she wanted that person to stay in touch, and kept the rest plain. Do not default to
   friendly filler. Calibration note: aim for roughly 10% warmer than a pure
   just-the-facts register. A light human opener or a plain-spoken aside ("there's
   plenty we can use," "worth lifting for our own material") is welcome. Gushing,
   exclamation points, and enthusiasm-signaling are not.

## §2 — Structure patterns

**Weekly Goals emails to Jeevan** follow a fixed three-section shape:
1. Things I did last week
2. Open questions / topics for Jeevan (3 items max, conversational)
3. This week (priorities, not a meeting list)

Rules for these:
- Workstream buckets: Fundraising, Strategy, BD, Research, AI, Other. (China folds into
  BD unless it is heavy enough that week to stand alone.)
- Sub-bullets throughout. Never paragraphs.
- Lead with wins ("Got into NVIDIA Inception," "Finally heard back from General
  Catalyst"), do not bury them.
- The forward section is actual work priorities, not a list of meetings.
- Consolidate related work into one tight bullet rather than itemizing every thread.
- AI section stays lean: outcomes and next steps, no tooling or setup minutiae.

**Slack / Teams / quick replies:** shorter than you think. One to three sentences.
Structure only if there is more than one moving part. No greeting-and-sign-off scaffolding
for a two-line message.

**Short internal notes and memos:** bullets or short blocks. If it runs long, it is
probably tracker content wearing a memo costume. Split it.

## §3 — Mechanics (hard rules)

- **No em dashes. Ever.** Use a period, comma, colon, or semicolon. This is a standing
  rule for all of Becca's output, not a preference.
- **No bold for emphasis** inside internal prose. Structure and placement signal
  priority. (Bold section labels in a bulleted list are fine: "Fundraising:", "BD:".)
- **Active voice.** Name the actor. "Greg will review the contract," not "the contract
  will be reviewed."
- **Cut adverbs.** No "really," "just," "actually," "genuinely," "honestly," "simply,"
  "truly," "literally," and no -ly softeners or intensifiers.
- **Specifics over vague declaratives.** "The Open Targets endpoint rate-limited all
  session" beats "we hit some technical issues."
- **Vary rhythm.** Do not stack three short punchy sentences. Do not end every bullet
  the same way. Two items often beat three.
- **Lazy extremes** (every, always, never, everyone, nobody) usually overstate. Use the
  specific number or scope.

---

## §4 — Openers and closers to cut

Delete throat-clearing. State the content.

Cut these openers: "Here's what," "Here's the thing," "I wanted to reach out," "Just
following up," "I hope this finds you well," "Quick note to say," "I'll be honest,"
"The truth is," "At the end of the day," "Circling back," "Moving forward."

Cut these emphasis crutches: "This matters because," "Make no mistake," "Full stop,"
"Let that sink in," "Needless to say," "It goes without saying."

Cut meta-commentary: "Let me walk you through," "As you'll see below," "In this note I
will," "I just want to flag that" (flag it, drop the preamble).

Replace business jargon with plain words: navigate → handle; unpack → explain; deep dive
→ analysis; circle back → return to; on the same page → aligned; lean into → accept;
game-changer → significant.

Closers: Becca does not thank people for their time as filler, does not ask them to let
her know if they have questions, and does not tack on an upbeat coda. A message ends when
it ends. Sign-off is a name or nothing, matched to the thread.

---

## §5 — Structures to avoid

These are AI tells that break the voice. State the point directly instead.

- **Binary contrasts:** "not X, it's Y," "the question isn't X, it's Y," "X isn't the
  problem, Y is." Drop the negation. Say Y.
- **Negative listing:** "Not a memo. Not a plan. A decision." Say the last thing.
- **Dramatic fragmentation:** "One target. That's it. That's the pitch." Write the
  complete sentence.
- **Rhetorical setups:** "What if we," "Here's what I mean," "Think about it." Make the
  point.
- **False agency:** "the decision emerges," "the data tells us," "the conversation moved
  toward." Name the person. Greg decided. I read the data. Jeevan steered the call.
- **Narrator-from-a-distance:** "People tend to," "This is why." Put the reader in the
  seat: "you," "we," the specific team.

## §6 — Dictation cleanup mode

When Becca pastes or dictates raw stream-of-consciousness and asks for it in her voice:

1. Extract the actual asks and decisions. Everything else is context she was talking
   through, not content for the output.
2. Sort by workstream if it is an email; by priority if it is a to-do list.
3. Move tracker-detail out. If she mentioned a specific VC conversation, a financial
   assumption, or presentation logistics, those go in a "for the tracker, not the email"
   aside at the bottom, or get cut. Do not smuggle them into the body.
4. Keep her phrasing where it is already tight. Do not rewrite a good direct sentence
   into a smoother, blander one.
5. Return the clean version. Flag anything you dropped so she can pull it back.

## §7 — Quality check (run before returning any draft)

| # | Check | If no |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Could a busy colleague get the point from the first line or first bullet? | Move the point up |
| 2 | Any em dash anywhere? | Remove it |
| 3 | Any throat-clearing opener or filler closer? | Cut it |
| 4 | Any adverb or vague declarative? | Cut or make specific |
| 5 | Is it structured (bullets/buckets) where there is more than one thread? | Add structure |
| 6 | Did any tracker-level detail leak into an email body? | Move it or cut it |
| 7 | Is the warmth level matched to the relationship, not defaulted? | Adjust |
| 8 | Is it shorter than a likely first draft? | Cut |
| 9 | Is this internal? (If external Sego prose, wrong skill.) | Switch to sego-writing-style |

If it feels long, it is.

## §8 — Before / after

**Rejection note**

Before (too much): "Thank you so much for taking the time to interview with us! We were
really impressed by your background, but after a lot of careful thought we've
unfortunately decided to go in a different direction at this time. We wish you the very
best of luck in your search."

After (Becca): "Thanks for the time you spent with us. After careful consideration, we've
decided not to move forward at this time. I wish you the best with your search."

**Slack ping to the team**

Before: "Hi all! Just wanted to circle back on the CI tool. Here's the thing: I think we
should really lean into getting the tracker built out first before we move forward with
anything else. Let me know your thoughts!"

After: "On the CI tool: let's get the tracker built before anything else. It's the source
of truth the memos hang off. Kirstin, can you take the build?"

**Goals email bullet**

Before: "I spent a significant amount of time this week working through the various
fundraising responses that were due, including making sure the Avego and Polaris diligence
materials were fully addressed and complete."

After: "Fundraising: finalized Avego and Polaris diligence responses. CurieBio draft
next."

---

## §9 — Handoff

If the request turns out to be external Sego prose (a diligence answer, an investor memo,
a BD pre-read, board or regulator material), stop and use `sego-writing-style`. That skill
is answer-first and comparative by design, which is the right register for a skeptical
external reader and the wrong one for an internal note. This skill and that one do not
overlap; pick by audience.
