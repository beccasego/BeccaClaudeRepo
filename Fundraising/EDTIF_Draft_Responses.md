## Q18. Overview of market dynamics (size, growth projections, etc.)

Sego develops cancer drugs for solid tumors, a global market worth over $200 billion in 2025 and projected to exceed $300 billion by 2030. Cancer remains the second leading cause of death worldwide, and new drug categories continue to capture share from older chemotherapy regimens. Sego's specific approach is radioligand therapy (RLT), a newer treatment category that delivers radiation directly to tumor cells using a precision-guided molecule while sparing healthy tissue. The RLT segment generated ~$2.6 billion in 2025 and is projected to reach $4.8–10.7 billion by 2030, growing at 10–13% annually. Only two RLT drugs have received U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approval today, both sold by Novartis, and the lead product alone generated $1.4 billion in revenue in the first nine months of 2025 (up 33% year-over-year). Four RLT companies sold for a combined $8.9 billion since 2023, confirming that large pharmaceutical companies view this treatment category as a major growth area.

## Q19. Primary competitors and relative market share

The broader solid tumor oncology market is served by every major pharmaceutical company worldwide, including Merck, Roche, Bristol Myers Squibb, AstraZeneca, and Eli Lilly, each generating tens of billions in annual oncology revenue. Within Sego's specific category (radioligand therapy), Novartis holds over 90% market share through its two approved RLT products for prostate cancer and rare hormone-secreting tumors. No other company has an approved RLT drug. Three large pharma companies have entered the space through acquisitions: Bristol Myers Squibb ($4.1B), AstraZeneca ($2.4B), and Eli Lilly ($1.4B). Several venture-backed startups are also testing RLT drugs in human studies, including Aktis Oncology and Alpha-9 Oncology. Sego competes on different ground than all of these: the company targets cancer types where no RLT product exists today, using a proprietary molecule design and radioactive isotope pairing that no competitor has replicated.

## Q24. Decision criteria for this project

Three criteria drive the decision to build in Utah rather than a coastal biotech hub. First, isotope logistics: Sego's drugs use radioactive materials that lose potency during shipping (they decay to half strength in 7–10 days). Co-location with Nusano's isotope production facility in Salt Lake City eliminates transit losses — no other US location offers this. Second, speed to job creation: manufacturing in-house cuts ~12 months of outsourcing delays, accelerating hiring of manufacturing, quality, and clinical operations staff in Utah. Third, cost efficiency: Sego's internal manufacturing runs $6.4M over two years vs $15M outsourced, a 57% savings that the company reinvests into Utah-based hiring and capital expenditure rather than paying out-of-state contractors.

## Q41. Why Utah is a strategic advantage

Utah gives Sego four advantages that no other US location can replicate. First, Nusano's isotope production facility shares Sego's building in Salt Lake City — eliminating shipping decay for radioactive materials and providing a domestic supply source independent of foreign producers. Second, Huntsman Cancer Institute and the University of Utah sit within 30 minutes and offer clinical trial infrastructure, research collaboration, and a pipeline of trained scientists that would take years to replicate elsewhere. Third, Wasatch Group, a Utah-based investor, committed $20M in equity plus funded the facility build, creating long-term local capital alignment that frees Sego from dependence on out-of-state venture fundraising cycles. Fourth, Utah's quality of life, cost of living, and outdoor access help Sego recruit specialized scientists who might otherwise default to coastal biotech hubs — a meaningful edge when competing for a small national pool of radiochemists and nuclear pharmacists.

## Q44. Why this incentive is critical

The EDTIF's post-performance structure aligns with Sego's growth plan: the company only receives a tax credit after creating new high-paying jobs and paying state taxes on those wages. That alignment makes the incentive low-risk for the state and high-impact for Sego. Sego faces a critical 18–24 month period between facility completion (December 2026) and its next major fundraise (mid-2028), when the company is investing heavily in Utah hiring and manufacturing before institutional venture capital arrives. Knowing the EDTIF credit will partially offset the state tax burden on new hires lets Sego commit to faster headcount growth in Utah — targeting [BECCA — insert FTE target] employees — rather than staging hiring more conservatively. Without it, Sego would slow hiring or shift roles to contract positions outside Utah, reducing the state's tax base and job creation.
