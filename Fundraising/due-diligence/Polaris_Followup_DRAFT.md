## 1. Can Sego raise less and get through FIH with only two programs? How much would that cost?

We will have a data-driven answer to this question within 3 months, when initial screening results on all five targets are in hand. Until then, picking two programs is a hypothetical exercise rather than a capital allocation decision. Our hit ID campaigns on ACP3, KLK2, CEACAM5, and MUC1-C will read out over that period, with CLDN1 trailing due to transmembrane protein handling requirements. If the data forced a two-program path, the criteria would be (i) binder affinity and selectivity, (ii) full conjugate performance, (iii) clinical positioning vs existing programs, and (iv) indication diversification, likely one prostate and one non-prostate target.

We are running the detailed cost model for a two-program FIH scenario as part of our LF1 budget process. We will share that analysis when complete. The directional answer: narrowing to two programs extends per-program runway but does not change our conviction that Phase 0 human dosimetry on multiple targets is the highest-EV use of seed capital. OncoACP3 was acquired by BMS for $1.35B and Aktis was valued at $1.2B at IPO, both on Phase 0 dosimetry of a single lead compound. A multi-target Phase 0 dataset strengthens the Series A negotiating position beyond what a single-program path can deliver.

## 3. Strategic value of integrated manufacturing

Sego's Stage 1A manufacturing facility is a value-creating asset, not overhead. Internal production saves approximately 57% versus CDMO outsourcing over the first two years of clinical operations ($6.4M internal vs $15.0M CDMO for 4 Phase 0 + 1 Phase 1 programs). The breakeven point is taking more than one molecule to clinic. Sego plans to take at least two.

The strategic case extends beyond cost. Radiopharmaceutical acquirers place a premium on integrated manufacturing capability. Fusion ($2.4B), RayzeBio ($4.1B), and POINT Biopharma all had internal manufacturing at acquisition. Mariana Oncology and 3B Pharmaceuticals did not, and transacted at materially lower valuations. In our BD conversations, Roche, Novartis, and AbbVie have each flagged manufacturing infrastructure as a differentiator in their evaluation of potential partners. The CDMO alternative adds approximately 12 months to program timelines through tech transfers and scheduling constraints. Sego's single-site model, adjacent to Nusano's isotope production facility, eliminates decay-in-transit losses, simplifies the regulatory filing, and gives the team direct control over clinical supply from Phase 0 onward.

The facility build cost is approximately $3M net of Wasatch's TI contribution, with GMP equipment structured as a lease rather than capital expenditure. The shared HVAC, shielding, and waste handling infrastructure serves both the clinical manufacturing suite and the preclinical hot laboratory, amortizing the investment across all programs. Stage 2 expansion (Phase 3 and commercial capacity) is deferred to Series A.

## 4. Seed raise benchmarking and value trajectory

Sego's $80M seed is sized to reach two Phase 0 FIH readouts with human dosimetry data, the inflection point that has driven the largest value creation events in the radiopharmaceutical sector over the past three years. The raise is in line with median seed and early-stage financings for comparable radiopharmaceutical platform companies. Aktis Oncology raised through IPO at a $1.2B valuation on Phase 0 dosimetry of a single lead compound. RayzeBio's OncoACP3 program drove a $1.35B acquisition by BMS, again on Phase 0/Phase 1 data. Fusion Pharmaceuticals reached a $2.4B acquisition on early clinical evidence. Sego's seed is designed to generate the same class of data across two targets, with a third potential program from in-licensing. The multi-target approach diversifies binary risk while building a dataset that positions the Series A ($150M, targeted mid-2028) on transactable clinical evidence rather than preclinical projections.
